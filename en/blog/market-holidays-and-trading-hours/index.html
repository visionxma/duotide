Market Holidays and Trading Hours: What Changes in Each Market

Rafael AzevedoEditorial character · Markets Desk

Quick answer

Each market has its own calendar: stock exchanges close on the holidays of the exchange's country, forex runs from Sunday night through Friday, and cryptocurrencies trade every day. Even when the market is open, holidays and weekends usually have lower volume and a wider spread. Before trading near a holiday, check the instrument's calendar on the platform.

A common mistake for beginners is treating the financial market as a single thing, with one schedule. In practice, each asset class follows its own calendar. The stock exchange closes on its own holidays. Forex runs from Sunday night through Friday. Cryptocurrencies trade around the clock. Gold, oil, and futures indices follow the calendar of the exchange where they trade.

Understanding these differences avoids two traps: trying to trade an asset that's closed, and, more dangerous, trading an asset that's technically open during low-liquidity hours, with a wider spread and sharper moves. In this guide we use real daily quote records between October 2025 and October 2026 to show what actually changes on these days.

What market holidays and trading hours are

A market holiday is the day when the exchange responsible for an asset doesn't open the session, or opens with reduced hours. Trading hours are the window in which orders are accepted and executed. Both depend on the country and the exchange, not your personal calendar: a Brazilian national holiday doesn't close the American exchange, and the reverse is also true.

How each market behaves

Stocks: the exchange's fixed calendar

Stock exchanges close entirely on their calendar's holidays and, on some dates, have a shortened session. In S&P 500 data between October 2025 and October 2026, there was no trading on November 27 (Thanksgiving), December 25, January 1, January 19, February 16, April 3 (Good Friday), May 25, June 19, July 3, and September 7. On the Ibovespa, the closed days were different: November 20, December 24, December 25, December 31, January 1, February 16 and 17 (Carnival), April 3, April 21, May 1, June 4 (Corpus Christi), and September 7.

Forex: open almost always, but not always liquid

The currency market is global and decentralized. It usually runs from Sunday at 5 p.m. to Friday at 5 p.m., New York time. When one country is on holiday, other centers keep operating. In EUR/USD's daily records, the only business days with no quote in the period were December 25 and January 1.

Cryptocurrencies: 24 hours, every day

Crypto assets trade without a break, including weekends and holidays. But open doesn't mean liquid: participation drops quite a bit on these days, as the numbers below show.

Commodities and futures indices

Gold, oil, and futures indices follow the futures exchange where they trade. In the gold contract's daily records, the days with no close were the same as the S&P 500's, because the contract follows the American calendar.

Real example: what the data shows

EUR/USD on American holidays

Between October 1, 2025, and October 7, 2026, EUR/USD's average daily range (high minus low, divided by the open) was 0.458%. On the eight American holidays when the pair had a quote, the average was 0.306%, about two-thirds of normal. On February 16 the range was 0.196%; on May 25, 0.212%. The exception was June 19, with 0.502%, above average: a holiday doesn't guarantee a quiet market.

Bitcoin on weekends

Between April 1 and October 4, 2026, BTC/USDT's average volume on Binance was 9,700 BTC a day on Saturdays and Sundays, against 18,824 BTC on business days: a bit over half. The average daily range also fell, from 3.39% on business days to 1.92% on weekends.

Bitcoin at Christmas 2025

On December 18, 2025, volume was 25,405 BTC. On Christmas Day it fell to 7,097 BTC, and on December 27 and 28 it stayed at 4,470 and 4,446 BTC. On the business days between the holidays volume rose again (18,345 BTC on December 26 and 19,895 BTC on December 29), but January 1, 2026, stayed at 6,280 BTC. These numbers describe what happened that year, not what will happen next year.

How to use the calendar in your favor

Check the instrument's calendar

Before a week with a holiday, check the asset's calendar inside the platform, not your own country's calendar. An American index contract stops on the American holiday, even on a business day in Brazil.

Watch for daylight saving time

In 2026, the United States moved its clocks forward on March 8 and back on November 1; Europe did so on March 29 and October 25. During these mismatched weeks, the time difference between London, New York, and Brasília changes, and the overlap window between sessions, which concentrates much of forex's liquidity, shifts. Check your platform's times after each change.

Extended hours

Trading stocks before the open or after the close can be useful to react to news, but volume is much lower, the spread is wider, and the order may be filled far from the price on the screen.

Limitations

Lower liquidity doesn't necessarily mean smaller moves. With fewer participants, a large order or unexpected news can move the price harder, and on the reopening after a holiday there can be a gap that jumps past the stop.

Common mistakes around holidays and outside trading hours

  • Assuming every market follows Brazil's holiday calendar.
  • Keeping a short-term trade open through a holiday closure without planning for it.
  • Ignoring the wider spread on low-liquidity days.
  • Trading crypto on the weekend with the same position size as on business days, without looking at volume.
  • Forgetting American and European daylight saving time when planning forex trades.

To practice without risking money, use a demo account during a week with a holiday: note the spread and the range of the asset you follow on the holiday and on normal days. Logging those numbers a few times shows, for your own market, how much conditions change and whether it's worth trading on those days.
