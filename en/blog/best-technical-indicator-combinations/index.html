3 Technical Indicator Combinations That Complement Each Other

Duotide Team · 09/24/2026 · 6 min read

After learning technical indicators separately, it's natural to want to use several at once to gain more confidence in an entry. The problem is that stacking similar indicators on the same chart usually produces the opposite effect: repeated signals, visual confusion, and a false sense of security. An efficient combination isn't the one with the most lines on screen, but the one that brings together tools from different categories, each answering a distinct question about the market.

This article presents three combinations that cover different questions — direction, strength, and volatility — and shows how to read them together, without repeating the same information disguised as a new indicator.

The golden rule: never combine two indicators from the same family

Technical indicators can be grouped into families: trend ones (moving averages, MACD), momentum ones (RSI, stochastic), and volatility ones (Bollinger Bands, ATR). Using two moving averages with similar periods, for example, is almost the same information repeated twice. The gain in precision comes from crossing different categories, because each one views the market from a different angle.

Combination 1: moving average with RSI

The 50-period moving average helps identify the price's predominant direction: above the average, bullish bias; below, bearish bias. The RSI (relative strength index), in turn, measures whether the asset is too stretched upward (above 70) or downward (below 30). The combined reading works like this: in an uptrend (price above the average), wait for the RSI to pull back near 40-50 before considering a buy entry, instead of buying right when the indicator is already near 70, a sign the move may be running out of short-term steam.

Combination 2: MACD with Bollinger Bands

The MACD indicates momentum changes through the crossover between two averages. Bollinger Bands show volatility: when they narrow, the market is compressed and tends toward a stronger move soon; when they widen, volatility is already high. A useful reading is watching for a bullish MACD crossover happening right after a period of narrow bands — this usually indicates a directional move is starting to gain strength, not just more noise within a sideways range.

Combination 3: short- and long-term moving averages with ATR

The crossover between a fast average (for example, 9 periods) and a slow average (21 periods) signals short-term trend changes. The ATR (average true range) doesn't indicate direction, but the average size of the asset's movement, which helps calibrate the stop loss. Suppose an asset with a daily ATR of R$ 1.20: a stop placed just R$ 0.30 from the entry will likely get hit by normal market noise, even if the trade's direction is correct. Using 1.5 to 2 times the ATR as the minimum stop distance usually gives the trade enough room to breathe.

How to test a combination before actually using it

  • Choose at most two or three indicators from different categories.
  • Note, over a test period, how many times the combined signals coincided with a favorable move.
  • Assess the result over at least 20 to 30 simulated trades before drawing any conclusion — too few examples favor chance.
  • Drop indicators that rarely agree with each other in your preferred time window.

What to avoid

Avoid adding a fourth or fifth indicator just because an isolated signal seemed weak. The more filters you stack, the fewer trades will satisfy all of them at once, and the practical result is usually letting good opportunities pass while waiting for a perfect confirmation that rarely shows up. No combination of indicators eliminates risk — they help organize the chart's reading, but the market remains subject to news and sudden changes that no indicator anticipates.

In the end, the best combination is the one you understand deeply and can apply consistently, not the most sophisticated one. Choose a pair of indicators from different categories, test it on a demo account for a few weeks, and only then consider using real capital with a risk management plan already defined. Review the combination periodically: a pair of indicators that worked well in a market with a clear trend can lose effectiveness once the asset enters a more sideways phase, and adjusting that reading over time is part of the process for any trader.
