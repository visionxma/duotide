Nike (NKE) stock in 2026: real recovery or value trap

Quick answer

Nike (NKE) stock fell 46.1% in 2026 through October 7, pressured by falling sales, a roughly 20% contraction in China, tariffs, and excess inventory. Even with the price much lower, the P/E stayed above 20, because earnings fell too. The recovery depends on margin, inventory, and China; until then, the risk of a value trap stays on the radar.

Few brands are as well known as Nike, but in 2026 the company's stock went through a very difficult year: a sharp price decline, contracting sales, and a recovery plan still underway. The question dividing analysts was direct: was Nike facing a real recovery, or a value trap, where the price looks low but the problems have not been solved yet?

This article gathers the numbers released about Nike (ticker NKE, on the New York Stock Exchange) in mid-2026 and uses the stock's actual daily chart, with the MACD, to show what happened from February to October. The business data reflects that moment. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell.

What Nike does

Nike designs and sells footwear, apparel, and sports equipment under the Nike, Jordan, and Converse brands. The core of the business is footwear, where performance sneakers also function as everyday fashion items.

The company sells through two channels: its own stores, apps, and websites, where it controls price and brand experience, and a network of wholesale retail partners, which secures shelf space and global reach. In recent years Nike had concentrated sales in the direct channel, and in 2026 it began reversing part of that strategy.

The numbers released in 2026

  • Annual revenue of $46.3 billion, down 9.8% year over year.
  • Company guidance for a revenue decline of 2% to 4%, with a contraction of about 20% in Greater China.
  • Trade tariffs adding about $1.5 billion in extra costs in 2026 and compressing gross margin by about 130 basis points.
  • Excess inventory estimated at $7.7 billion, a target of the restructuring plan called "Win Now," with a goal of returning to a gross margin above 42%.
  • Free cash flow of $3.3 billion and earnings per share (EPS) of $1.52 for the 12 months through June.

Why a low price doesn't mean a cheap stock

NKE closed 2025 at $63.71 and, on October 7, 2026, stood at $34.36, down 46.1%. Even so, the P/E did not come down, because earnings fell too. With the EPS of $1.52 cited in June:

  • Close on June 30, 2026: $41.05 ÷ 1.52 = P/E of 27.0.
  • Close on October 7, 2026: $34.36 ÷ 1.52 = P/E of 22.6.

A P/E above 20 after a drop of nearly half the stock's value shows that the market was still paying for a recovery that had not yet shown up in the numbers. That is exactly the argument of those who call it a value trap.

Real example: Nike's MACD in 2026

The chart shows NKE's daily candles (Yahoo Finance) and the MACD (12, 26, 9), with the MACD line, the signal line, and the histogram. Between February and October there were 14 crossovers between the two lines. What happened after them teaches a lot about indicators in a downtrend.

February: the bearish signal that worked

On February 27 the MACD crossed below the signal line, with the stock at $62.18. Ten trading sessions later it stood at $53.98, down 13.2%. The signal tracked the stock's main direction.

March and April: the bullish signal on the eve of the biggest drop

On March 31 the MACD crossed above the signal line, with the stock at $52.82. The next day, April 1, NKE fell 15.5%, to $44.63, the year's biggest single-day drop. The MACD reached the period's lowest point, −4.08, on April 10, with the stock at $42.62.

June to October: a series of bullish signals, without a rally

Of the period's seven bullish crossovers, six ended with the stock lower ten trading sessions later: among them, June 9 (−6.3%), July 29 (−6.3%), and September 24 (−4.5%). On October 2 the stock made the period's low, $31.97 intraday, and closed at $33.87, the year's lowest close.

How to analyze Nike stock

Greater China and inventory

The recovery depends on sales in Greater China stopping their decline and on excess inventory shrinking without burning margin through discounts. Both numbers show up in every quarterly report.

Gross margin and tariffs

The target of a gross margin above 42% is the thermometer of the "Win Now" plan. Trade tariffs work against that target, and changes in U.S. trade policy directly affect profit.

Wholesale and the sports calendar

The return of strength in the wholesale channel and the major sporting events of 2026, including the World Cup, were cited as opportunities to recover volume, especially in soccer and running.

Limitations

The MACD is calculated from moving averages and lags. In a long downtrend, bullish crossovers appear on every small bounce and fail in sequence, as in 2026. The P/E above uses June's earnings, which change every quarter.

Common mistakes when analyzing Nike stock

  • Assuming a stock is cheap just because it fell a lot, without looking at the P/E and the earnings trajectory.
  • Trusting the strength of the brand while ignoring inventory, tariffs, and the decline in China.
  • Buying every bullish MACD crossover in a clear downtrend.
  • Ignoring the MACD's position relative to zero: below it, the medium-term bias stays bearish.
  • Expecting sporting events alone to solve margin and inventory problems.
  • Reading the 2026 chart as a forecast: it shows what happened, not what will happen.
