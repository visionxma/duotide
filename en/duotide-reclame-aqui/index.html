Duotide on Reclame Aqui

This page does not repeat a score or a complaint count for Duotide. The reason is simple: that data changes every week, and a site that publishes an outdated reputation number is misinforming you. What we do here is show how to look it up and — more importantly — how to interpret what you find.

Where to check

  • Reclame Aqui (reclameaqui.com.br): search the exact name and any variations;
  • Consumidor.gov.br: the official government platform, with formal company responses;
  • CVM (cvm.gov.br): authorization lookup and the market alert list;
  • A simple search: the broker's name plus "withdrawal", "won't pay" or "blocked my account";
  • Forums and groups: raw accounts, useful for detail, but unverified.

The most common reading mistake

Few complaints does not mean a good reputation. A foreign broker with no Brazilian CNPJ often does not even have a registered page on Reclame Aqui. An empty chart can mean no registration, a small customer base, or a recent company — none of that is a compliment.

The reverse also holds: a large, reputable broker racks up complaints simply by having many customers. The absolute number says little; what matters is the proportion of resolved cases and, above all, what people are complaining about.

What kind of complaint is a serious warning sign

  • Withdrawal not processed or repeatedly postponed — the most serious of all;
  • Account blocked right after a withdrawal request;
  • Demand for an extra deposit to release a withdrawal — never legitimate;
  • Support that stops responding after a withdrawal request;
  • Unilateral change to bonus rules after the deposit.

A complaint about platform slowness or a trading loss is noise: the first is a common technical issue, and the second is usually market risk, not a broker failure.
